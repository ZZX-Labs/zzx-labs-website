# FreedomKobraCEX Licensing and FreedomX Partnership Policy

## Classification
The requested mandatory branding, territory, and attribution conditions mean this public license is **source-available**, not OSI-approved open source. A true OSI license cannot honestly be combined with a rule forbidding branding removal or restricting deployment territory.

## FreedomX Community Source License 1.0 — project policy
Community users may inspect, study, modify, self-host, and build from the source provided that:

1. FreedomKobra attribution and the national satire layer remain visibly present in public deployments.
2. The official United States edition is represented as the United States edition.
3. Bitcoin remains the only cryptocurrency in an official FreedomX deployment.
4. Modified builds do not falsely claim FreedomX/ZZX-Labs audit or government endorsement.
5. Security/noncustodial notices are not removed in a way that misrepresents custody.

## Branding waiver
Material removal or replacement of FreedomKobra branding requires a separate written commercial license defining duration, entity/domain scope, geography, support, audit obligations, Bitcoin-denominated fee, renewal, and attribution level. No fixed fee is embedded here.

## Full attribution removal
Complete removal of FreedomX/ZZX-Labs attribution is the highest-control tier and requires a separately negotiated, time-limited contract at the highest applicable Bitcoin-denominated commercial rate.

## Other countries
India, Nigeria, Jamaica, and other jurisdictions should build culturally specific national FreedomX ports rather than relabel the U.S. or Canadian edition. A candidate national port should retain Bitcoin-only scope, noncustodial keys, equivalent-or-better security, local legal review, compatible schemas, and independent security review. After review and contract it may receive FreedomX project certification.

Certification means project-network compatibility/security/branding approval. It is not government approval or a financial license.

## True open-source alternative
If FreedomX later adopts an OSI license such as AGPL-3.0, branding restrictions should move to trademark/certification policy. Forks could then remove branding in code but could not falsely use protected marks or claim official certification.
